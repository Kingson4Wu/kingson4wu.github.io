## Findings Applied

- Added course metadata: PHIL 176, Yale College course, Spring 2007 recording, twice weekly 50-minute meetings, 26 official lectures.
- Added professor title: Shelly Kagan, Clark Professor of Philosophy.
- Added main texts and representative course-packet authors: Plato, John Perry, Tolstoy, Hume, Thomas Nagel, Parfit, Bernard Williams, Swift.
- Softened absolute phrasing around religious authority: the course's arguments do not rely on religious authority, but the course is not framed as a general attack on students' religious beliefs.
- Softened Kagan's physicalism claims from "proves souls do not exist" to "argues for physicalism / challenges arguments for immaterial souls."
- Softened "supports the modest existence requirement" to "leans toward it as a comparatively acceptable option"; Lecture 18 explicitly notes that it still has counterintuitive implications.
- Softened personal identity conclusion: the body/brain theory is attractive, but the course's later emphasis moves to "what matters," especially psychological/personality continuity.
- Clarified fear of death: Kagan distinguishes fear of dying, early death, illness, pain, and uncertain harms from fear of inevitable nonexistence itself.
- Corrected Makropulos reference to Elina Makropulos / E.M.

## No Major Issue Found

The article's main structure matches the official Open Yale course: metaphysics first, value theory second, then death's badness, immortality, fear, life planning, and suicide rationality/morality.
